6. Important Things to Understand Before Building
There are three major challenges in your idea.
A. How will your website find people who want to switch jobs?
This is the first problem to solve.
A website cannot reliably determine whether a person wants to change jobs merely by finding their name online.
You need an appropriate source of candidate information, such as:
- Candidates who register directly on your website.
- Professional profiles available through authorized APIs.
- Recruitment databases that permit your intended use.
- Candidate records provided by an authorized recruiter.
For your first version, build your own candidate database with voluntary registrations and fictional demo records.
Later, explore third-party integrations.
B. Can you automatically send emails to everyone?
Technically, email automation is possible. However, the application should not indiscriminately email every person it discovers.
Use approved recipient lists, appropriate outreach permissions, sending limits, and unsubscribe handling.
For an actual HR business, the rules depend on where the candidates are located, where the recruiter operates, how the contact information was obtained, and the purpose of the message.
India's applicable privacy and communications requirements should be assessed before deploying the system commercially.
C. What about automatically collecting phone numbers?
Make mobile-number collection part of the candidate's response form.
For example:

1. A candidate receives an appropriate recruitment email.
2. They open the job opportunity.
3. They click "I'm Interested."
4. They verify their email.
5. They voluntarily provide their mobile number if they want a phone call.
6. The HR executive receives the completed candidate record.
This gives the recruiter a much more useful lead than simply collecting an email click.
7. What Would Make Your Project Stand Out to Top MNCs?
A basic recruitment website is a common student project. Your implementation and engineering decisions will determine how impressive it becomes.
I'd focus on these five features.
1. Reliable background processing
If a recruiter approves 500 emails, your backend should handle them through a queue rather than blocking a web request.
2. Duplicate prevention
The same candidate should not accidentally receive the same campaign repeatedly because of a retry or server error.
3. Secure authentication and permissions
A recruiter should only access candidate records they are authorized to view.
4. Explainable candidate matching
Show why a candidate matches a job instead of presenting an unexplained AI-generated percentage.
5. Meaningful analytics
Track the candidate journey from eligible contact to response and recruiter follow-up, while distinguishing email delivery from actual candidate interest.
These features demonstrate engineering ability r
ather than just frontend design.
8. How Could You Turn This Into a Startup?

Once you have a working prototype, you could explore offering it to small recruitment agencies and independent HR consultants.
Potential business models include:
Model	How it works
Monthly subscription	Recruiters pay for access to the platform.
Per-recruiter pricing	Agencies pay based on the number of recruiter accounts.
Usage-based pricing	Charges depend on campaign or workflow usage.
Agency plan	A higher tier provides team management, reporting, and integrations.


These are possible models, not a guarantee of market demand or revenue.
Before building a commercial version, interview several HR professionals. Find out how they currently source candidates, how they track interest, which tools they use, and what they would actually pay to automate.
Your contact who works as an HR executive could be a useful first person to interview and potentially test the prototype with fictional or properly authorized data.
Don't start by building every feature. Start by validating the biggest pain point.
